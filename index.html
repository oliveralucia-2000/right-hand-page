THE CEILING

Your business can’t grow past your own capacity.

You built a 7- or 8-figure company by being the person who could handle anything. That’s how you got here. It’s also what’s holding back the next stage.

Every decision waits for you. Every follow-up sits in your inbox. Every process lives in your head instead of in a system. The calendar fills up, the backlog grows, and the business moves at the speed of one person: you.

Being at the center of everything feels like leadership. It isn’t. It’s a structural problem. The business was designed around you, so it can only go as far as you can carry it.

Stop being the engine. Start being the owner.

  1. 1

    Doing

  2. 2

    Managing

    you hand off tasks, but you still track, chase and check every one of them.

  3. 3

    Delegating outcomes

    someone else owns the result, and you only see what actually needs you.

Doing

Most founders get stuck at step two. They’ve handed off the work but are still carrying all of it.

The framework sets a simple benchmark for the third stage: could you step away for 90 days and come back to a business that’s more efficient and more profitable than when you left?

That’s the standard the Delegation Framework measures against. It’s a test to build toward. It is not a promise, and it is not a reported client result.

THE HIRE PROCESS

About 1 in 1,000 applicants gets placed.

That figure describes how selective Pareto Talent’s own applicant process is: roughly 1,000 applicants for every Right Hand placed. It isn’t a ranking of talent worldwide, and it isn’t a client result. It’s the filter.

Right Hands are sourced from Latin America. They’re selected through the HIRE Framework, a seven-step, trial-based system from the book HIRE by Kasim Aslam and Ivan Bunin:

Select a step to read it.

STEP 1 OF 7

Clarify core principles

define what great looks like before anyone applies.

  1. 1

    Clarify core principles: define what great looks like before anyone applies.

  2. 2

    Create the job posting: written to attract the right people and turn away the wrong ones.

  3. 3

    Distribute strategically: put the role where top operators actually look.

  4. 4

    Filter ruthlessly: cut early and often.

  5. 5

    Run paid trial projects: candidates prove themselves on real work, not just in conversation.

  6. 6

    Interview finalists: only after the work has spoken.

  7. 7

    Onboard for success: set up the placement to start strong.

The flytrap. Hidden inside the process is a deliberate test, such as a specific subject line, a problem-solving wrinkle or a missed deadline. It’s designed to catch whether a candidate truly pays attention. Operators who miss it don’t move forward.

The HIRE Framework’s own stated benchmark is about 3 hours of founder time per hire and an 80%+ success rate. It compares that with 40–60 hours and roughly 50% for traditional hiring. (These figures are claims from the published methodology, not results reported by Right Hand clients.)

Source: HIRE by Kasim Aslam and Ivan Bunin; paretotalent.com

THE ROLE

Not a task list. An owner.

A Right Hand is a full-time, elite remote operator who takes ownership of the areas that keep pulling you back in:

  • Inbox & calendar what reaches you, and when
  • Follow-ups nothing slips because you didn’t get to it
  • CRM the pipeline stays current without you updating it
  • SOPs processes leave your head and become documented systems
  • Automations repetitive work gets built out of the business
  • Marketing support the execution that keeps campaigns moving
  • Customer success clients stay looked after
  • Projects owned from start to finish

Tasks vs. outcomes.

Task

Completing a task means doing what you asked.

Outcome

Owning an outcome means making sure the result happens: spotting what’s missing, following up, and closing the loop without being told.

QUESTIONS

Before you book.

Most hiring relies on résumés and interviews. The HIRE process uses paid trial projects, so candidates prove themselves on real work before they ever reach the interview stage.

Selectivity is the point. There are around 1,000 applicants for every placement, a hidden attention-to-detail test, and a role designed around owning outcomes instead of waiting for instructions.

Start with what keeps pulling you back in: inbox, calendar, follow-ups, CRM, SOPs, automations, marketing support, customer success and projects. The Delegation Framework maps the path from doing, to managing, to delegating outcomes.

Kasim’s argument is that AI isn’t coming for your job; it’s coming for your rigidity. Ivan Bunin, co-author of HIRE, puts it this way in his AI-Powered EA Framework: mediocre talent plus AI equals scaled mediocrity, and extraordinary talent plus AI equals exponential results. A Right Hand is the person who puts AI to work for your business.

An executive assistant completes tasks. A Right Hand owns outcomes. You stop managing the work and start receiving results.
